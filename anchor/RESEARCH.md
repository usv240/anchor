## 2. The key study: health care in barbershops

**Victor RG, Lynch K, Li N, et al. "A Cluster-Randomized Trial of Blood-Pressure Reduction in Black Barbershops." *N Engl J Med* 2018;378(14):1291 to 1301. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1717250. PMID 29527973.**

| | Plain words | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Can barbershops help men get their blood pressure under control? | Cluster-randomised trial. Shops, not people, were assigned by chance. |
| Who | 319 Black men with high blood pressure in Los Angeles County | Systolic BP 140 mm Hg or higher, from 52 Black-owned barbershops |
| What happened in the intervention group | Barbers encouraged customers to meet pharmacists **inside the shop**. The pharmacists could prescribe medicine, working with the men's doctors. | Specialty-trained pharmacists under collaborative practice agreements |
| What happened in the control group | Barbers encouraged healthy habits and doctor visits. | Usual-style health promotion |
| Result | **63.6%** of men in the barbershop-pharmacist group reached a healthy blood pressure, against **11.7%** in the control group. | BP under 130/80 at 6 months, P < 0.001. Systolic fell 27.0 mm Hg vs 9.3 mm Hg; adjusted difference 21.6 mm Hg (95% CI 14.7 to 28.4). 95% retention. |
| Why it matters to Anchor | The setting, a trusted place people already visit, was central to the design. | |

## 3b. Impact: what it could prevent, and why it is stuck (added Oct 17)

**Headline numbers, in the order the landing page uses them:**

| Number | Meaning | Source | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5.4x** | 63.6% vs 11.7% of men reached a healthy blood pressure | Victor et al., *NEJM* 2018 | Measured |
| **About half the strokes** | 49% fewer strokes if the 21.6-point drop lasts | Our calculation from Ettehad et al. 2016 | **Estimate** |
| **684,000** | Adults in LA County with high blood pressure and no medicine | Our calculation from CDC PLACES 2025, all 2,474 tracts | Model-based estimate |
| **894** | Licensed barbershops and beauty salons inside the 20 highest-need zones | City of LA business licences (CC0), NAICS 812111 and 812112; City of LA only, so a minimum | Count |
| **1 city** | The programme has not spread beyond the original trial | Kohrman et al., *PLoS One* 2024 | Reported |

**What lowering blood pressure prevents.** Ettehad D, Emdin CA, Kiran A, et al. "Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Lancet* 2016;387(10022):957-967. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8. 123 studies, 613,815 participants. Relative risk per 10 mm Hg lower systolic BP:

| Outcome | Per 10 mm Hg (measured) | Scaled to 21.6 mm Hg (our estimate, RR^2.16) |
|---|---|---|
| Stroke | 0.73 | 0.51, about **49% fewer** |
| Heart failure | 0.72 | 0.49, about **51% fewer** |
| Major cardiovascular events | 0.80 | 0.62, about **38% fewer** |
| Coronary heart disease | 0.83 | 0.67, about **33% fewer** |
| All-cause mortality | 0.87 | 0.74, about **26% fewer** |

Assumptions, stated on the site: the drop is sustained, and risk falls log-linearly with blood pressure (the review found proportional effects across baseline levels). These are not Anchor results.

**Why it is stuck.**
- Kohrman N, Rashid M, Flores R, et al. *PLoS One* 2024;19(5):e0303075. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0303075. Qualitative follow-up of the LA barbershop trial (31 interviews). It reports that the programme "has not expanded beyond the original clinical trial setting", and that a familiar, trusted setting was key to success.
- Chong S, Huynh B, Wong S, et al. *Health Equity* 2023;7(1):835-842. doi:10.1089/heq.2023.0157. In a small survey (17 Black male barbershop patrons), 47% would prefer health care in barbershops, 56% in pharmacies and 53% at home. So the right venue differs by person and community. This is the gap Qloo's taste data addresses.
- A cost-effectiveness analysis of the LA programme was not found in Europe PMC on Oct 17. Do not claim cost-saving until one is verified.

**The pitch in one line:** "A way to prevent strokes that works 5 times better than usual care is stuck in one city, because nobody knows which local places to take it to next. Anchor tells you where."

## 4. What the evidence does NOT show (stated openly)

1. **The barbershop result came from pharmacists plus barbers, not the venue alone.** Anchor finds venues. The outcome still depends on the health programme that shows up there.
2. **The trial was in one group (Black men) in one city (Los Angeles).** Whether other venue types (cafes, gyms, record shops) work as well for other groups has not been tested in a randomised trial. Anchor's job is to make those venues findable so programmes can try and measure them.
3. **No study has tested whether data-driven venue selection beats local knowledge.** Our evaluation compares Anchor with named baselines, but the only real proof would be a field trial. We say so on the site.
4. **CDC PLACES values are model-based estimates** (from the BRFSS survey plus Census data), not clinical counts. They are good for ranking neighbourhoods, not for counting patients.
5. **Qloo affinity measures what a group likes, not foot traffic.** We treat it as a signal of cultural centrality and report how often it agrees with real partner venues (see the plan).

## 5. The gap Anchor fills

The research says: go where people already gather and trust, and try more kinds of places. Health departments know the *need* (from data like CDC PLACES) but not the *trust*: which specific local places a given group of residents loves. Today that knowledge lives in the heads of a few local staff, and it does not scale across a county of about 2,500 census tracts.

Anchor joins the two:

- **Need:** CDC PLACES, for every census tract, gives the estimated share of adults with high blood pressure, and the share of those taking medicine.
- **Trust:** Qloo, for any area and age-and-gender group, gives which places that group loves, with explanations.
